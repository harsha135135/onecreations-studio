# SHOWCASE v1 — best controlled cinematic action shot (built 2026-10-03 → 2026-10-06)

> **THIS IS A SHOWCASE OPTIMISATION RUN, NOT A CONTROLLED ADMISSION EXPERIMENT.**
> Characters, look, lighting, framing, resolution, compositing and effects were optimised together for final visual
> quality. Nothing here isolates which change caused which improvement, and no capability conclusion in the M2/M3-A
> documents is changed by it. No PASS/FAIL is assigned: the result is a **SHOWCASE RESULT**.

Figures are **measured** unless labelled otherwise. ₹0 spent, nothing downloaded, no training, no cloud. Run
telemetry is in `admission_records/showcase/` (kept apart from the admission ledger), media on `G:\onecreations`.

## 1. Starting state

The starting point was commit `028bb66`: M2-C PASS, and M3-A at CONTROL PASS / APPEARANCE PARTIAL / overall PARTIAL.

Ranked visible weaknesses of the M3-A output, checked on its frames (`renders/m3a_action/analysis/ref_vs_raw_f23.png`):

1. **Characters read as toy mannequins:** spheres and cylinders with ball joints, a box torso, a segmented ascetic.
2. **Flat, bright, beige daylight look,** with no low-key drama and weak separation.
3. **Diagnostic composition:** small actors, a lot of floor, and saturated green/magenta/cyan tracking banners.
4. **Staging:** the demon faces the camera, not the defender.
5. **Fast-swing weapon:** it breaks in f25–27; the raw mace becomes an orange blob.
6. **Plain temple:** box shrine, plain pillars, no motivated light.
7. **Small impact:** a dust-only impact.

Items 1–4 and 6 were fixed in the authored inputs before any generation. Item 5 is handled by the hybrid layer and
item 7 by deterministic effects.

## 2. Character improvements

New `integrations/blender/oc_characters.py` (bpy + stdlib + numpy, procedural):

- **Metaball anatomy:** ellipsoid elements are given as visible semi-axes (visible radius = 0.575 × influence
  radius, measured). They fuse into one smooth mesh per body region.
- **Swept tubes:** Catmull-Rom paths with per-point radius and a root-to-tip UV, used for horns, tusks, ears, straps,
  hair locks, trident prongs and the gorget.
- **Armour shells and cloth panels with folds.**
- **Rest-space chain weights:** smooth blends between bones.

**Demon** (`sc_asura`):
- **Body:** one continuous torso, neck and head mesh with pectorals, abdominals, lats, trapezius, a heavy brow, a wide
  jaw, cheekbones, nostrils, deep eye sockets and a snarl. Continuous arms run from the deltoid to the fist.
- **Rig:** the M2-B rig and strike keys are used verbatim (`sc_asura_rig`).
- **Weapon fist:** weighted to the `mace` bone, so the grip always wraps the shaft. The wrist blends forearm → fist
  over z 1.06–1.12 m.
- **Legs:** a wide grounded stance, static and weighted to the pelvis, since the M2-B legs are never keyed.
- **Head details:** long two-tone ivory horns, tusks, pointed ears, emissive amber eyes, a black mane of 7 locks and a
  topknot with a bronze ring.
- **Costume:** layered iron pauldrons with spikes (on the upper arm), crossed leather chest straps fitted to the body
  surface by ray casts, a bronze medallion, a belt and buckle, front and back loincloth, leather tassets, iron
  greaves, bronze anklets, leather bracers and bronze armbands.
- **Skinning check:** 98,347 vertices, **0 unweighted**, max weight-sum error 9e-5, recorded in the spec and
  Blender-tested.

**Mace:**
- a flanged and spiked aged-gold head with a top spike
- an iron shaft with a leather grip, bronze collars and a pommel

**Defender** (`sc_defender`, static, no rig):
- an ash-blue ascetic in a forward spear guard, with the trident aimed at the attacker and held in the image plane
- face structure (sockets, eyes, brow, cheekbones, lips), a wide matted bun and 9 locks
- a white dhoti with a pleated front drape, a pale shawl, an ochre sash and rudraksha beads, fitted to a torso-only
  proxy

**Staging:**
- the demon's root is yawed 30° toward the defender (root yaw only; the strike stays a sideways arc in the image)
- the defender stands at (0.70, −3.55), yaw −65°, scale 1.08

## 5. First frame, resolution, controls

**Resolution and aspect:** 768×448 (aspect 1.714), chosen on the M2-B evidence: admitted, sharper static detail and
+16 % time. The weapon is protected deterministically anyway. The camera and framing were composed for 1.714
directly; nothing is a crop of a 512×320 frame.

**First frame:**
- a separate 1024-sample Cycles render of frame 1 (`render_reference.py --first-frame-samples 1024`, 17.2 s)
- the same scene, camera and frame as the passes, so the alignment is exact; it is the appearance anchor
- SHA-256 `fe9850d0…`

**Controls:**
- **Depth:** per-clip min–max disparity, lossless, from the 1-sample geometric passes (range 4.90 → 25.9 m).
- **Exporter improvements:** volume-only helpers are hidden (haze), plus a new `oc_exclude_from_passes` flag for the
  semi-transparent brazier flames, recorded in `passes.excluded_objects`. Only 14 isolated background pixels remain
  in all 49 frames.
- **Rubble:** the first rubble pass read as eggs and was replaced by angular rocks before generation.

**Pre-generation gate:**
- **Passed:** joints, timeline, camera contract and clearance.
- **Not valid for this look:** the colour trackers. In the warm high-contrast grade, the stone and shrine shadows
  fall in the gold and crimson hue ranges: `gold_deep` fires on about 13,000 shrine pixels per frame, and `crimson`
  on the maroon banners. Colour-based retention, timing and position diagnostics are therefore not used for this
  shot. The control inputs are validated geometrically.

## 6. LTX configuration, steps, seed

**Workflow:** `workflows/ltx25/showcase_v1_api.json` is the admitted 768×448 graph (`m2b_strike_768_api.json`) with
only the prompt and the output prefix changed:
- INT8 ConvRot 22B distilled + Union IC-LoRA 1, guide 1, first-frame 1
- `euler_ancestral`, CFG 1, 49 frames at 24 fps

**Prompt:** appearance only. It describes the new designs; the word-boundary test found no camera or choreography
words.

**Steps: 8.** The model card (`G:\onecreations\models\ltx\LTX-2.5\README.md`) specifies the distilled checkpoint as
a "Fixed 8-step schedule, CFG=1". Extra steps would need sigmas outside its distillation schedule, so 12 or 16 steps
were not tried. The dev transformer, which supports variable steps, is not downloaded.

**Seed: 1000.** There is no comparable-condition evidence for any other seed, so there was no seed sweep.

**Upscale:** none possible. The LTX latent spatial upscaler was never downloaded (models.yaml `not_downloaded`), and
ComfyUI's `upscale_models` / `latent_upscale_models` folders are empty. A plain Lanczos resize would add no detail, so
the deliverable stays at native 768×448.

## 9. Weapon repair (hybrid)

The final rule was refined after viewing run 1; post-hoc refinement is allowed for a showcase. It extends the
reusable compositor and `CompositeManifest`.

1. **Weapon owned by Blender in all 49 frames.** `protection.include_frames` covers the whole shot, so there is no
   switch from the raw mace to the authored one mid-shot.
2. **90° shutter for the weapon layer** (0.25 frame; matched blur reference kept at 180°). The authored head peaks at
   90 px/frame (1.27 head widths). At 180° it renders almost transparent in f25–26, which would excuse a missing
   head; at 90° the streak is about 22 px and the head stays solid.
3. **Holdout occlusion:** every non-layer mesh is a holdout, so the hand, body and dust occlude correctly.
4. **Grade:** a per-channel gain fitted from raw pixels under the layer's solid core in slow frames: [1.075, 1.070,
   1.337].
5. **Softening:** `soften_px` 0.7, a premultiplied Gaussian, so the crisp Cycles weapon matches LTX's softness.
6. **Ghost suppression, `ghost_mode: swept_band`.** Colour can't separate the weapon here (§5), so the region is
   geometric:
   - which frames: only the authored fast frames f23–29
   - where: the authored weapon silhouette dilated by 8 px, outside the layer's solid core, never over the moving
     demon
   - `ghost_fill: temporal`: each pixel is taken from the nearest raw frame where it is weapon-free, aligned by a
     ±6 px shift matched on a background ring (RMSE ≤ 22), with diffusion only for leftovers
   - **result:** f23–25 and f28–29 were filled from neighbouring frames (73–272 px diffused per frame). f26–27
     (about 6,000 px each) fell back to diffusion: with the static defender allowed into the fill region, no source
     matched under the RMSE limit. At these frames the fill lies mostly under the opaque head and streak, and a
     side-by-side with the earlier variant (defender protected, temporal sources found) shows no visible difference
     (`analysis/keep_compare_f25_28.png`).
7. **Attachment:** the projected grip lies within 0.3–2.7 px of generated crimson hand pixels in f23–29.

## 10. Impact effects (deterministic, ActionTimeline-triggered at output f29)

**`impact_fx`** (20 frames, f29–48, 90° motion blur):
- dust volumes: a 16-sphere plume and a 14-sphere ground ring, noise-shaped, with density keyed 1.6·e^(−t/0.6 s), a
  light backlit albedo, faint warm self-light and 2 volume bounces
- 18 angular stone chips on ballistic arcs that come to rest on the floor
- 12 sparks for 5 frames
- a persistent crack and crater decal from the contact onward

**Revisions:** a first dust version rendered as near-black smoke and was revised twice after viewing.

**`impact_flash`:** a light-only pass (new `LayerRender.light_only`). Only the flash point light is on; world and
emission are off; the film is opaque. It is composited additively (`blend: add`) over 5 frames at 2,200 / 1,100 / 420
/ 140 / 40 W, so the floor, legs and haze light up on the beat. The first version (5,000 W) blew out the defender and
was cut to about 45 %.

**Post jolt:** a 2-px camera response at f29–31, applied in post only (the authored camera and control are untouched).

## 11. Compositing and final grade

**Compositing:** `tools/admission/hybrid_composite.py` combines raw run 1 with the mace layer, then `impact_fx`
(over), then `impact_flash` (add) → `composite/hybrid.mp4` (lossless).

**Finish** (`tools/admission/finish_grade.py`, new, reproducible; parameters and filter chain in
`admission_records/showcase/…_finish.json`):
- the post jolt
- `eq` contrast 1.05
- `colorbalance` cool shadows / warm highlights ±0.03
- vignette (angle 0.45)
- luma unsharp 0.35
- fixed-seed luma grain 3
- outputs: a lossless master and an H.264 crf-12 view file

## 14. Subjective assessment (SHOWCASE RESULT)

**This is clearly the strongest complete video the project has produced.** Against M3-A at matched beats:
- the characters are continuous creature and ascetic designs, not jointed primitives
- the lighting is low-key, warm/cool cinematic with haze and fire
- the weapon reads as a solid, attached, directionally blurred mace through the whole swing
- the impact is a synchronised flash, dust, sparks, debris and cracks
- nothing in frame is a diagnostic asset

The authored control is visibly intact: timing on the beat, articulation, orbit and parallax, and occlusion. It reads
as a stylised CG fantasy action beat, not a technical demo. It does not reach frontier-model photorealism.

## 15. Remaining weaknesses

- **Stylised CG characters:** muscular proportions, smooth skin, a blobby metaball look up close, the defender's
  dhoti reading partly like trousers, and simplified faces. LTX reproduces the first frame's design and adds no
  realism.
- **LTX softness:** fine texture is lost; there is no upscaler.
- **f26 fast-swing head:** a fuzzy, splashy streak, with a small pink-red raw fleck near the trident tip for one
  frame. f26–27 ghost fill is diffusion (see §9).
- **Static defender:** no reaction to the impact, and no contact or parry.
- **Unauthored doorway fire:** a generative change, harmless here.
- **Colour diagnostics:** not usable under this grade. Future showcases need geometric or temporal analysis tools.

## 16. The one change that would most improve the next showcase

**Higher-fidelity character surfaces in the first frame:** sculpt-level anatomy and costume, such as subdivided
retopologised meshes with displacement detail and painted or procedural skin. These would replace metaball blobs,
because LTX faithfully carries the first frame's design and that design is now the appearance ceiling.

A defender reaction (one authored flinch or brace on the contact beat) would be the next most visible gain.
