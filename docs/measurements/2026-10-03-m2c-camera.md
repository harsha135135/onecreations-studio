# M2-C — authored camera move in the representative temple — 2026-10-03

**Question:** can the Blender → depth → LTX-2.5 Union pipeline preserve an authored cinematic camera trajectory, and the parallax it produces, in a representative scene?

**Answer for this test: M2-C = PASS** (one generation, no retry).

All figures are **measured**, single observation, unless labelled otherwise.

**Generated viewpoint evidence is image-space only:** landmark trajectories, parallax, framing and occlusion. **No camera pose is inferred from the generated video**, so no metric or angular camera error is claimed for it. The authored camera is known exactly from Blender and the export.

## 1. Experimental isolation

**One new axis:** static camera → **authored moving camera**.

**Kept from the proven baseline:**
- the M2-A temple environment (same builders, materials and lighting)
- the M2-A rigid humanoid proxy (same geometry and materials)
- 512×320 × 49 frames at 24 fps, seed 1000
- the graph: INT8 ConvRot + Union IC-LoRA 1, 8 distilled steps, `euler_ancestral`, CFG 1, guide strength 1, first-frame strength 1

**Removed:** articulation, the strike, fast thin-object motion, contact, physics and VFX. No 768×448.

**Workflow:** `workflows/ltx25/m2c_camera_api.json` is the M2-B/M2-A graph with only the output prefix changed.

## 2. Scene choice and diagnostic additions (`integrations/blender/build_temple_camera_scene.py`)

**Scene:**
- the M2-A temple
- `m2c_asura`, the M2-A proxy, at (+2.0, −3.5), right of centre, so the whole left colonnade stays clear of the actor during the move
- the static ascetic `m2a_shiva`, report-only

**Diagnostic additions (documented, not artistic):** three flat cloth banners in colours absent from the M2-A scene, hung on the shafts of the **left colonnade**:

| Landmark | Colour | Pillar | GT depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| NEAR | green, 0.5 × 1.3 m | front pillar (−3.2, −2.0) | 8.3–8.8 m |

- Each banner is a separate object with its own pass index, which gives ground truth.

- **A VERY FAR landmark was not practical.** In ray-cast probes of the authored move, the back wall stayed hidden behind the shrine and the tall actor, and the shrine tower ended above the actor's head.

**Landmark selection:**
- **Process:** per-frame Blender ray-cast visibility probes of candidate points (pillar faces, walls, shrine) under the authored move, run before any generation.
- **Superseded attempts:** two earlier diagnostic layouts were rejected before the gate. In one, the subject was centred and the far layer swept behind it. In the other, the far landmark was a shrine-tower banner that sat in the tower's shadow (rendered RGB 39/94/86, below the cyan rule) and was approached by the actor's horns late in the move.
- **Kept:** the final layout is the one recorded here. No generation used the earlier layouts.

**Subject:** static, **option B** of the brief. The subject has no keys, so all scene motion comes from the camera; this is cleaner than reusing M2-A's translation. It is the M2-A proxy unchanged: gold mace and pauldrons, crimson skin.

## 3. Camera trajectory (authored; `CameraMotion` `m2c_orbit_push_in_v1`)

**Move:** an orbit around the actor's chest (2.0, −3.5, 1.55) plus a push-in, from a moderately raised position.

| | Start (frame 1) | End (frame 49) |
|---|---|---|
| Azimuth around the target | −13° | +11° (**24° orbit**) |
| Horizontal distance to the target | 7.5 m | 6.0 m (**1.5 m push-in**) |
| Height | 3.6 m | 3.3 m |
| Position (m) | (0.313, −10.808, 3.600) | (3.145, −9.390, 3.300) |
| Pitch (look-at) | ≈ 15.3° down | ≈ 16.3° down |

**Shape and timing:**
- **Easing:** 50 % linear plus 50 % cosine ease-in/out (no speed ramps).
- **Translation:** **chord 3.18 m, path 3.22 m** over 2.0 s; speed mean 1.61 m/s, peak 2.07 m/s.
- **Rotation:** heading change about 24°; peak angular speed 15.4°/s; **roll 0** (≤ 4e-5°). Kinematics come from `camera.analysis.trajectory_stats`.
- **Framing:** every frame looks at the target, then turns 12° left (a constant yaw framing offset), so the actor holds the right third (u ≈ 364–370 px).
- **Why raised (proposed, from the probes):** at 2.4 m the far layer was always behind the actor or the colonnade. The raised camera shows the colonnade's depth ladder over the floor.

**Intrinsics:** **fixed 35 mm** lens, 36 mm sensor, AUTO fit, 512×320, clip 0.1–300 m. There is no focal-length animation.

**Representation:** Session A infrastructure throughout:
- `geometry.conventions.look_at` builds each pose
- `camera.trajectory.CameraTrajectory` is saved as `camera_motion.npz`, one sample per frame at t = k/24
- the `CameraMotion` manifest, `camera_motion.json`, is validated with the contract and pins the trajectory SHA-256
- the Session A importer `oc_import_camera.apply` keys it onto `OC_Camera` (LINEAR, one key per frame)
- `oc_export` exports it

The samples are also recorded as CSV with `camera.io_csv`: `admission_records/…_camera_samples.csv`.

## 6. Interpretation rules, pre-registered in the spec before the run

The spec's `classification` and `tracking` sections hold the rules.

**PASS requires all of:**
- every landmark's `trajectory` followed:
  - direction cosine ≥ 0.9
  - magnitude 0.6–1.6× GT
  - checkpoint error ≤ max(16 px, 0.3 × GT displacement)
  - monotone progress (no backward step > 0.1)
  - no jump: a single-frame step ≤ max(3 × the largest GT step, the largest GT step + 12 px)
  - progress within 0.25 of GT at the checkpoints
- `parallax`: the order equals GT, with pairwise ratios within ×/÷1.35
- framing followed: `position` within 32 px and `approach` gain ≥ 50 % of GT
- visual agreement that the clip reads as an authored viewpoint change rather than morphing

The PARTIAL and FAIL definitions follow the owner's brief. The occlusion/reveal timeline and appearance are report-only.

| NEAR | (37, 84) | (41, 78) | (50, 68) | (64, 58) | (73, 53) | (+35.8, −30.8) → 47.3 px | 0.994 | 0.905 | 7.4 px (limit 16) | 0.899 |
| MID | (84, 71) | (95, 66) | (115, 57) | (137, 49) | (151, 45) | (+66.8, −25.7) → 71.6 px | 0.998 | 0.902 | 9.0 px (limit 23.8) | 0.900 |

- **Direction:** the generated viewpoint moves the same way as the authored one at every landmark.
- **Parallax order and ratios:** reproduced almost exactly. Normalised **0.457 / 0.692 / 1.0** against GT 0.454 / 0.691 / 1.0. The pairwise ratios are 1.516 (GT 1.520), 2.189 (2.202) and 1.444 (1.448), so the ratio error is ≤ 0.6 %.
- **Magnitude:** every layer reaches **≈ 90 %** of its authored displacement. The progress lag grows smoothly to 0.10 by the last frame, identically for all three depths. The model rendered a slightly shorter move, about 0.9× and uniform. It did not distort the depth structure.
- **Temporal progression:** monotone at every landmark (worst backward step 0.0016 of the total); the largest single-frame step is 3.6 px, against a 15 px jump limit. There are no stalls, reversals, jumps or cuts.

## 9. Subject framing

- **Position:** the generated centroid stays at u ≈ 355–358 (GT 364–370). Displacement from frame 0 is within **7.7 px** of GT at every checkpoint. The constant tracker offset is 16–22 px because the gold parts aren't red.
- **Push-in:** area grows ×1.498 against GT ×1.575, about 87 % of the authored growth, consistent with the ≈ 0.9× move. It is non-decreasing, above the pre-registered threshold of 1.287.
- **Result:** `position` and `approach` are followed. The authored "actor held in the right third while approaching" behaviour is reproduced.

## 10. Occlusion and reveal ordering (report-only)

**Far banner:**
- In Blender it is partly behind the third pillar's shaft at the start and is **revealed** as the camera orbits. Its visible area goes from 77 % to 99 % of its maximum by f16, with partial occlusion in [0–11].
- The generated clip shows the same early reveal: tracked relative area 0.82–0.88 over f1–7, then ≥ 0.92 from about f10.
- Later frames show apparent "hidden" dips (from f25). These are tracker dropouts from slight colour drift, not occlusion. The generated cyan's green-to-red ratio drifts from 2.94 (reference) to 2.77, so the strict mask loses edge pixels. A loose cyan criterion still finds 85–89 % of the reference banner area through f48, and a zoom confirms the banner is intact and unoccluded.
- The pre-registered hidden-frame IoU (0.205) is therefore not informative here.

**Ascetic:** it is never more than half hidden in GT. The blue rule catches only about 150 of about 1,700 px under this lighting, so it adds no reveal evidence.

**Visual:** the pillars' mutual overlaps (the shafts separating along the colonnade) change in the same order as in the reference.

## 11. Geometry coherence and visual observations

- **Geometry:** pillars stay straight cylinders, and banners stay attached to their pillars and keep their shape. The floor-tile perspective rotates consistently with the reference. The shrine, steps and ascetic stay in place relative to the architecture. There is no duplication, bending or texture sliding at the checkpoints.
- **Rigid-viewpoint signature:** the identical 0.9× progress at all three depths is what a single coherent viewpoint would produce. Layer-wise morphing would make the depths progress inconsistently.
- **Temporal stability:** smooth. The subject is consistent (M2-A look; slightly softer).
- **Appearance:** the far banner's cyan drifts slightly; the image is softer than the reference overall.
- **Cinematic usefulness:** a believable high-angle orbit and push-in at this envelope.
- **Static-structure drift:** omitted. It is not meaningful under a moving camera.

## 15. Conservative conclusion

At the tested 512×320 / 49-frame configuration, LTX-2.5 Union depth conditioning preserves a moderate Blender-authored camera move (24° orbit plus a 1.5 m push-in, fixed lens, high angle) strongly enough to reproduce coherent viewpoint progression and depth-dependent parallax in the representative temple scene:
- the direction is right at every landmark
- the relative parallax between three depth layers is reproduced within 1 %
- progression is smooth and monotone
- the subject framing follows

The generated move reaches about 90 % of the authored displacement, uniformly.

## 16. Remaining limitations (unproven)

- **Single observation:** one move, one seed, one scene, 49 frames.
- **The ≈ 0.9× magnitude:** it is unexplained. It could be the model, the control's temporal compression or the first-frame anchoring.
- **Not shown:**
  - arbitrary or long paths
  - FPV, aggressive moves, roll, whip pans or focal-length changes
  - landmarks nearer than the orbit pivot (they would move the opposite way)
  - moving-camera combat
  - exact camera-pose reproduction, which can't be measured from the generated video
- **Appearance:** small, saturated diagnostic markers drift in colour.
